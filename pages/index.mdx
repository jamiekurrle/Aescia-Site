# Post-discharge monitoring that fits real clinical workflows

**Aescia supports structured patient follow-up after discharge — helping clinical teams identify risk earlier and respond through consistent, auditable escalation pathways.**

Patients deteriorate after they leave. Current follow-up is often unscalable, inconsistent, or absent for the patients who need it most. Aescia addresses the gap between discharge and the next scheduled clinical contact.

## What is Aescia?

Aescia is a post-discharge monitoring platform. It collects structured, symptom-based check-ins from patients following hospital discharge, assesses responses using clinician-authored rules, and classifies cases by clinical urgency. The result is a prioritized worklist for the clinical team — not an uncontrolled alert stream, and not an autonomous decision.

Aescia is **not** an autonomous diagnostic or triage system. Every output is advisory. Every action is taken by a clinician.

---

## Why it matters

### Earlier detection
Complications after discharge frequently go undetected until patients return to emergency. Structured follow-up creates a systematic signal where one does not currently exist.

### Fewer avoidable returns
Identifying early deterioration enables timely clinical response — phone review, GP referral, or outpatient escalation — before the situation requires an unplanned presentation.

### Safer discharge, better flow
When clinical teams have confident, structured visibility of post-discharge patients, they can discharge earlier with appropriate safeguards, supporting better bed utilisation without compromising patient safety.

## How it works

```
Patient receives daily check-in
        ↓
Responses assessed against clinician-authored rules
        ↓
Cases classified by urgency tier
        ↓
Clinical team reviews prioritized worklist
        ↓
Clinician acts — escalates, reviews, or continues monitoring
        ↓
All actions logged and auditable
```

## Built for hospitals

Aescia is designed for post-discharge pathways where structured follow-up currently doesn't scale:

- **Cardiothoracic and general surgery** — wound complications, respiratory issues, early cardiac events
- **Heart failure** — fluid status, weight change, symptom progression
- **Respiratory discharge** — breathlessness trajectories, saturation monitoring
- **High-risk medical discharge** — symptom monitoring across complex cohorts

## Why clinical teams trust it

- **Clinician-authored rules** — escalation logic is defined and owned by clinicians, not black-box algorithms
- **Auditable by design** — every classification, action, and outcome is logged
- **Governance-aware** — designed to operate within existing hospital governance and privacy frameworks
- **No autonomous decisions** — outputs are advisory; clinicians retain clinical responsibility at all times

## Frequently asked questions

**What exactly does Aescia do?**
Aescia collects structured patient-reported symptom data following hospital discharge, assesses responses using clinician-defined rules, classifies urgency, and presents a prioritized worklist to the clinical team. Clinicians review and act on each case.

**Is Aescia an autonomous diagnostic system?**
No. Aescia does not make autonomous diagnoses, triage decisions, or treatment recommendations. All outputs are advisory. Clinical responsibility remains with the treating team at all times.

**What stage is the company at?**
Aescia is an early-stage company preparing for its first clinical feasibility evaluation, expected to begin in 2026 subject to site governance and ethics approvals.

**Is Aescia already deployed in hospitals?**
Aescia is not yet in routine clinical deployment. Initial evaluation is being planned in collaboration with clinical and governance stakeholders.

**What does a hospital need to start?**
An initial pilot requires a defined patient cohort, site governance and privacy approvals, a nominated clinical lead, and a defined escalation pathway. Deep technical integration is not required to begin. [See full pilot model →](/implementation)
